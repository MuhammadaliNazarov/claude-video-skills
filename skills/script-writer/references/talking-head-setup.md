# Talking-head setup: background + light (small dorm or outside)

Recommend a setup with every script that has him on camera. If he sends a photo or a 5 s test clip of the spot,
check it against the list below and say exactly what to move.

## The 6 checks (in order)

1. **Light on the face, from the front-side.** Face the window (or a lamp) at about 45° to one side, so one cheek is a
   little brighter: shape, not flat. The window is the best free light in a dorm.
2. **Background darker than the face.** The face should be the brightest thing in frame (about one stop brighter than
   the wall). Never sit with the window behind him (silhouette, blown sky).
3. **Depth: the camera sees the longest line of the room.** In a small room, put the phone in one corner and sit
   about 1-2 m in front of the opposite corner, shooting diagonally across the room. Sit away from the wall (at least
   1 m); a wall right behind the head looks flat and boxed in.
4. **Three layers:** something soft in the foreground edge (a plant, a lamp, the bed frame), him, and a background
   with 2-3 deliberate objects (books, a poster, a red practical light: the brand colour).
5. **Clean the frame:** no laundry, no cables, no bright screens, no busy patterns behind the head; turn the overhead
   ceiling light **off** (it lights from the top, eye sockets go dark, the room goes flat).
6. **Camera at eye level, 1x lens,** about an arm-and-a-half away (chest-up framing), phone on a stack of books or a
   tripod, 4K, exposure locked on the face.

## Dorm recipes (small room)

| Situation | Setup |
|---|---|
| Day, one window | sit facing the window at 45°, phone between him and the window (slightly off-axis); background = the darkest corner; lamp off |
| Evening / night | one lamp (warm, with a shade or bounced off the wall) at 45° front-side at head height; a small **red LED or lamp** in the background corner for depth (on brand); everything else off |
| Very cramped | shoot from the doorway or the corner toward the window-side corner; sit on the desk chair turned 45° to the lens; keep the bed out of frame or made and plain |
| Desk / study vibe | laptop off-frame or dimmed, books + a lamp behind on the desk, him leaning in at MCU |
| Cinematic (creative-video-editor) | dark room, one practical lamp from the side, red object in the background, a doorway silhouette for B-roll |

## Outside recipes

| Situation | Setup |
|---|---|
| Best default | **open shade** (shadow of a building or tree, sky lighting the face): soft and even; background in sun is fine if it's not white |
| Golden hour (1 h before sunset) | sun behind and to the side of him (rim light on hair), face lit by the bright sky; never squint into a low sun |
| Midday sun | avoid; if you must, turn so the sun is behind him and expose for the face, or find shade |
| City / campus | stand 3+ m in front of the background (street, buildings) so it goes soft; walk-and-talk along the long line of a street |
| Wind / noise | a clip-on mic with a windscreen; turn the back to the wind; record a 10 s ambience take |

## Quick fixes

- Face too dark → turn toward the window/lamp; or move the light closer, not brighter.
- Hard shadows / shiny skin → bounce the lamp off a white wall or put paper/a sheet in front of it; blot the forehead.
- Background too bright → turn away from the window, close the curtain behind, or lower the exposure and add a lamp on the face.
- Looks flat → turn the light more to the side; turn the overhead off; add the background practical.
- Orange/blue mix → match light colours: daylight with daylight (lamps off by day), warm lamps at night.
- Echo in the room → bed, curtains, clothes in the room absorb it; clip-on mic close to the mouth.
