# Storytelling structures

Pick one structure per video. All of them obey the same three laws:
1. **Causation, not sequence.** Join beats with *but* (complication) or *therefore* (consequence), never *and then*
   (Trey Parker & Matt Stone's rule). Read the beats aloud; rewrite any "and then" joint or cut the beat.
2. **Open a loop, delay the close, close it.** The viewer stays to see a question answered. Close the main loop before
   the CTA; open a new one in the CTA ("follow to see what I got").
3. **Emotion is the engine, proof is the brakes on doubt.** An honest feeling (spite, fear, shame, relief) makes them
   care; photos, dates, screenshots and results make them believe.

---

## 1. Normal → Explosion → New Normal (the default story)

Used by most transformation and journey videos.

| Beat | What it is | Lines |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | where you were: the struggle, the pain, the status quo everyone expected | 30-40% |
| **Explosion** | the turning point: a decision, an event, a realisation ("until I met my orthodontist", "I decided I had enough") | 1-3 lines |
| **New Normal** | the payoff: wins, who you became, where you are now; end on the WIN | 30-40% |

Rules from the book:
- Start with a general pain point anyone can feel ("You need a jaw surgery"), even if they don't share it.
- Tell it on a specific timeline ("not even four months ago", ages 13 → 33).
- Show the struggle with concrete examples (rationing $30 for two weeks; two jobs, 72 h a week).
- Pick the story closest to your account's niche, so a viewer who likes it and visits your page follows.
- Long versions repeat "I failed at this / I failed at this" (keep it to three failures max).
- Don't force your life into someone else's exact lines; keep the structure, use your own beats.

**Expanded version (the transformation-authority script):**
pain point hook → how it made you feel (3-5 short lines) → set up the explosion ("I struggled alone / until I met…")
→ the wins after it → the character traits that changed ("And then… I perfected / I dared / I trusted") → the new
normal, focused on wins → "and we're only just getting started" → CTA.

## 2. The personal-intro story

Hook "This is [name]" → **context with tension + an authentic emotion** (spite, fear, excitement) that creates a
curiosity gap ("a 19-year-old unemployed dropout now", why?) → **explosion** (the decision) → **your niche/journey**
(now call out the niche specifically) → **goal/mission** (why you do it) → **connection** (a shared goal with the viewer:
"an army of book girlies who…"; the most important beat for converting to followers) → **CTA** (what following gets them).
Third person narration and a voice-over make it playful.

## 3. Kallaway's dopamine addiction loop (from the analysed Reel, ref01)

1. **Stakes:** make them care. Three ingredients: a character to root for, something at risk, urgency.
   ("I have to sell my house if I don't finish this mission in two weeks.") Stakes need not be life or death, only
   personally relevant to the ideal viewer.
2. **The big question:** enough context that a juicy question pops into their head ("will he actually hit 10k?").
   Stakes buy attention; the big question buys curiosity.
3. **The head fake:** set up assumption A, then snap the story to B. Contrast is what makes it addictive (mystery
   novels, twist endings).
4. **The re-hook:** as soon as one loop closes, deal the next hand. Good stories have one loop; great stories cascade
   loops in succession.

## 4. Kallaway's script format ("THE FORMAT")

Hook → **Lock-in zone** → Body block 1 → **Re-hook 1** → Body block 2 → **Re-hook 2** → Body block 3 (optional) →
Closing (optional) → **CTA with an aligned lead magnet**.

- *Lock-in zone* (interpretation, the image does not define it): the 2-3 lines right after the hook that confirm the
  viewer is in the right place, set the stakes and preview the payoff ("It's only four steps, but it uses the same
  psychology casinos use"). Name the thing, give the count, add authority.
- *Body blocks:* one idea each, with an example or analogy from a familiar world.
- *Re-hooks:* a line that opens the next loop before the old one is fully closed ("and this is really where you hook
  them", "but here's the part nobody mentions").
- *CTA with an aligned lead magnet:* the free thing offered is the natural next step of the topic ("Comment
  'storytelling' and I'll send the full video"), never a random freebie.
- In his words (podcast): a script is a dance of context and conflict, set-up then re-hook, not hook-body-conclusion.

## 5. Single-lesson story

Hook = the lesson stated as a contrarian rule ("You can't love someone for something and then get upset at them for
the same quality") → "Let me explain" → **one specific event** → the moment you caught yourself → **the takeaway** in
one sentence → CTA (send to your partner / comment agree or disagree). Remove every side detail.

## 6. Age-stamp / dialogue timeline

Text-only. Lines prefixed by age ("13: 'dad, why do we have to move to Australia?'"), voiced as a conversation with a
parent or as plain facts. The age stamps signal a payoff is coming. Normal (old, sad photos) → explosion (the
realisation: "Dad, you sacrificed so much…") → new normal (achievement photos, smiling) → a closing line of gratitude.
Real camera-roll photos with dates are undeniable proof.

## 7. Documented challenge

3x the stakes ("haven't been to class in 4 weeks, midterm in 3 hours") → the plan → each step, with one "the tip no
one mentions" → the moment of truth on camera → result (or "follow to see what I got" to open the next loop).

## 8. Promise → Proof → Plan (educational / authority)

Promise the outcome in the hook; prove you can deliver it (credential, result, experience: "I've been a flight
attendant for three years"); give the plan in steps; CTA. Proof can come first when the audience is cold.

## 9. Comparison / myth-vs-truth

Two characters or two columns (broke vs $10k, debit vs credit) alternating line by line, with ✗ and ✓ marks. Each pair
is a common myth followed by the better habit. Strongest point first.

## 10. List with personal proof (most Advice formats)

Hook names the number + who it is for + the outcome. Each point: the advice in one line + a one-line personal experience
that proves it worked. Unusual beats obvious. More points = shorter each; 3-5 points if you are challenging a belief.
End with an engagement question.

## 11. Story mining: L.I.F.E. pillars + Safe → Real → Raw (Jun Yuh)

Source: the user's notes from Jun Yuh, Kallaway and Vinh Giang videos (Gemini summary, 2026-10-13). Ordinary life is
enough; the depth comes from digging, not from big events.

- **Find the story in one of four pillars, L.I.F.E.:** **L**ove (relationships, friends, hobbies), **I**dentity (culture,
  upbringing, where you come from), **F**itness (mind, body, spirit, discipline), **E**arnings (career, skills, money,
  building). Example creator: Identity = hometown → new city, being the first/the different one; Earnings = studies,
  business, the startup; Fitness = discipline as a student; Love = family, friends, people met in each city.
- **Ask "why?" three times** before writing. Layer 1 **Safe** (the generic reason anyone would say) → layer 2 **Real**
  (the feeling underneath) → layer 3 **Raw** (the vulnerable truth you don't usually say out loud). Write from Raw.
  Example: "Why this university?" Safe: "great school". Real: "I wanted out of the life I was born into". Raw: "[ask the creator]".
  Never invent the Raw layer; ask for it.
- **Don't niche down to a topic, niche down to a person.** Jun Yuh's argument: a brand built on one rigid subject is
  "anti-human"; build it on the creator's perspective across all four pillars. (So the user can post across pillars as
  long as the lens is his.)

## 12. The 3 Ps: Problem → Pursuit → Payoff (Jun Yuh)

The simplest full arc; use it when normal → explosion → new normal feels too dramatic for the material.

1. **Problem:** lead with a relatable struggle. Struggle is the shared human ground that earns attention.
2. **Pursuit:** the unique method or journey of trying to overcome it: what *he* did that others don't (this is where
   the creator's perspective lives; make it specific and step-by-step).
3. **Payoff:** the outcome, insight or lesson. **It does not have to be a big win:** an honest realisation, a small
   step forward, or "still figuring it out" is a valid payoff, as long as the viewer leaves with something.

Map it onto the dopamine ladder: the Problem must open a high-stakes question (Captivation), the Pursuit drops
breadcrumbs and one head fake (Anticipation), the Payoff closes the loop with a non-obvious answer (Validation).

## 13. The Dopamine Ladder (Kallaway, six levels)

How a viewer moves from a scroll to a fan. Every script climbs levels 1-4; the channel climbs 5-6 over many videos.

| Level | What happens | How the script/edit does it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Stimulation (0-2 s) | the eye stops before the brain decides (bottom-up visual processing is ~200 ms) | the first frame has motion, colour, brightness or contrast: a moving shot, a bold red title, a face close to the lens. Never a static, dim, empty first frame |
| 2 Captivation | a curiosity loop opens | an open, high-stakes, relevant question; unknowns and extreme contrasts ("nineteen, from a small town, building a startup in a big city") |
| 3 Anticipation | the viewer starts guessing | give facts that point one way, then a **head fake** that resets the guess and raises curiosity |
| 4 Validation | the loop closes | the non-obvious answer, the payoff, the reward for staying |
| 5 Affection | the viewer starts liking the messenger, not just the message | show personality, passion, warmth, opinions; smile, posture, eye contact (Kallaway: likability is partly visual) |
| 6 Revelation | the viewer expects value from this person every time | consistency of value and identity across videos: same look, same lens, recurring series |

Consequence (Kallaway's argument): **faceless content tops out at level 4**; personality is what reaches 5 and 6. So
default to the user's face on screen at least once per video, even in text-on-screen formats (a clip of him, not only
B-roll of objects).

## Delivery and line craft

- Spoken pace 150-250 wpm; Kallaway runs 242 wpm with every pause cut. Calm reflective formats go
  slow on purpose: fewer words, shorter video.
- Sentences under ~15 words. Present tense. "You" language. One idea per line.
- Specific numbers in full when they are money ("a thousand dollars a month").
- Speak from conviction; record a message you genuinely believe (confidence is the hook).
- For text-on-screen videos, the music beat is the editor: reveals land on the drop, each line stays until you finish
  reading it aloud.


### Relive, don't report (Vinh Giang)

- **Be specific with the five senses + one emotion.** Not "I was nervous" but the click of the pen, the smell of
  stale coffee, the collar getting tighter. Sensory detail makes the listener simulate the moment. One or two senses per
  key moment is enough in short form; spend them on the explosion and the low point.
- **Present tense at the key moment:** "I'm standing in the airport back home, holding one bag…" instead of "I stood".
  Reporting is information; reliving is transformation. Use past tense for the setup, switch to present for the scene
  that matters. Delivery note for the editor/user: body and voice match the emotion of that line.
- **Say the meaning out loud.** The story is the doorway, the meaning is the room. Before the CTA, connect the dots in
  one line, e.g. "The reason I'm telling you this is…", "So here's what that taught me:". Short form can compress it
  ("Lesson: …" on screen), but never leave the viewer to guess why they watched.
- **Stories over bare facts:** a fact inside a story is far more memorable than the same fact alone (Giang cites a
  Stanford figure of 93% vs 13% recall; the commonly quoted Chip Heath classroom result is 63% vs 5%; either way the
  direction is the point, don't put the number in a script). Wrap every stat in a moment.
